# Clarity & Exposition — Round 1

**Recommendation:** minor polish
The document's through-line is sound and most prose is crisp, but two structural seams and one consistency gap are worth closing before it ships.

## Strengths

- The codebase-audit failure example in `02-motivation.prose.md` is the best piece of writing in the document. It earns its length: the failure is concrete, the silence of the failure is genuinely surprising, and the three-subagent resolution is immediate. "The agent produces a report. It looks complete." — that short pair of sentences lands.
- `03-content.prose.md`'s decision to put the mental model before the mechanics pays off. A reader who holds "orchestrator owns the problem, workers own the pieces" can predict what the code snippet is doing before they read it. The isolation paragraph ("This isolation is not a limitation — it is the design") closes a question the reader would naturally raise.
- The subagents-vs-teams comparison table is well-chosen as a decision tool. The two axes that matter (communication model, persistence) are the rows with the most explanatory weight, and they appear first.
- The conclusion's capability list matches `01-context` exactly — this is the right discipline and it closes a loop the reader feels even if they cannot name it.
- The spines are honest. Open questions in the spines are specific and resolved or named as genuinely open; the "Not doing" sections are precise rather than vague. That discipline keeps the prose from drifting.

## Weaknesses

- `01-context.prose.md` § *What subagents and teams are* — The framework survey sentence is a 97-word run-on listing seven frameworks in sequence. It reads as a catalogue, not an argument. The spine says "they are evidence of the pattern's breadth, not the subject," but the prose gives them equal weight with the definitions that precede it. A reader finishing the definitions paragraph hits this wall before they have been told why the frameworks matter.
- `03-content.prose.md` § *Teams: going further* — The prose introduces "task list" as if the reader already knows what it is: "Teams also share a **task list** — a shared queue of work items that teammates can claim, complete, and hand off without waiting for the orchestrator to assign each one." This is the first mention of the term and the definition is buried in a subordinate clause after the em-dash. The comparison table then lists it as a feature of teams under "Coordination" without repeating the definition. A reader who skims or uses the table as a reference will not find it explained there.
- `03-content.concepts.md` § *Open questions* — Both open questions in the spine are unresolved and neither is addressed in the prose. "Is the cost difference concrete enough without numbers?" — the prose leaves it as "Higher — each teammate is a full session," which is the same qualifier the spine was uncertain about. "Should the live demo slide have more scaffolding?" — the demo slide in the prose is three bare bullets with no framing of what the audience will see or what to watch for. The spine is accumulating unanswered intentions; the prose has stalled on both of them.
- `02-motivation.prose.md` § *When to reach for it — and when not to* — "The crossover point is not a fixed threshold — it depends on the task, the framework, and the token budget." This sentence appears in the prose and the same thought appears verbatim in `04-conclusion.prose.md` as an "open edge." Restating it twice without adding content between the two occurrences is redundant. The first occurrence should be the setup; the conclusion should say something the reader could not have said after section 02.

## Actionable

1. `03-content.prose.md` § *Live demo* — Add two sentences of scaffolding before the three bullets: what the audience will watch the orchestrator do, and one thing to notice that would be easy to miss (e.g., that the subagents' contexts never appear in the orchestrator's conversation). (*Why:* the demo is described by the spine as "the centerpiece"; three bare bullets do not signal that. Readers who have just absorbed the mental model need a frame to know where to direct attention during a live demonstration.)

2. `01-context.prose.md` § *What subagents and teams are* — Break the framework survey sentence into two: one short sentence naming the claim ("The same pattern appears across every major agentic framework, under different names"), then the catalogue reformatted as a compact list or shortened to three representative examples with "among others." (*Why:* a 97-word sentence in a definitions section trains the reader to skim, and skimming here means missing the orchestrator/worker definition that follows. The spine's intent — "evidence of breadth, not the subject" — is not realized when the sentence is longer than the paragraph it annotates.)

3. `03-content.prose.md` § *Teams: going further* — Move the task-list definition earlier in the section, before the spawn example, and bold **task list** at first use rather than after the em-dash. Then in the comparison table, make the "Coordination" row link back: "Self-coordinate through messages and shared task list (see above)." (*Why:* the term is introduced in the wrong order — after the reader has already seen "share a task list" in the spawn example without a definition. Introducing jargon before it is used is the standard; this section does it in reverse.)

4. `02-motivation.prose.md` § *When to reach for it* and `04-conclusion.prose.md` § *Open edges* — Cut the near-verbatim crossover-point sentence from section 02 and let the conclusion be the place it lands fully. In section 02, replace it with a forward reference: "Where that line falls is covered at the end of this tutorial." (*Why:* the current duplication wastes a finding — the conclusion could say something the reader could not yet say after section 02, but instead it just repeats the hedge. Forward-referencing from 02 to 04 also gives the reader a reason to keep reading.)
